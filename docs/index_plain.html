Abstract

A robot that offloads its policy to an edge server must stream camera images over a wireless link, and at low SNR or low rate the received images degrade until the policy fails. We observe that most of a manipulation scene is predictable: given the past frames and the robot's own pose, a video world model can forecast the arm, the table and the background, and only the parts it cannot foresee need the channel. We build a Wyner-Ziv semantic communication system around this idea. The receiver runs a pose-conditioned world model (Ctrl-World) that predicts the next camera latent; the transmitter, blind to this prediction, sends a few channels of a convolutional joint source-channel code of the true latent; and a residual decoder merges the two, using the prediction as decoder side information. The decoded image feeds an unmodified vision-language-action policy. In closed-loop simulation with PI0Fast over a CDL-C channel, at the same channel rate our system keeps 73–77% task success from 20 dB down to 12 dB and 57% at 8 dB, where the DVST video-transmission baseline fails completely.

Method

Prediction as side information. Ctrl-World predicts the next frame of both cameras from its own history and the robot's pose. The arm, table and background are predicted well; what it cannot foresee (where a cube really is after a grasp, whether the grasp held) must come over the channel.

Wyner-Ziv codec. The transmitter encodes the full latent with a fully-convolutional encoder that never sees the prediction, and sends the first k of 16 nested code channels as analog symbols. The receiver fills the unsent channels from a prior conditioned on the prediction and decodes a correction to it, given the received code, the prediction and the channel SNR. No masks or thresholds: the decoder learns how far to trust each source.

Training. The codec minimises a distortion that up-weights cells the predictor gets wrong, plus a light Wyner-Ziv rate term KL(q(s|zt) ‖ p(s|ẑt)), with the SNR drawn from 0–20 dB and nested channel dropout so any prefix of channels decodes.
